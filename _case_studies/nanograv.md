NANOGrav is an international collaboration that studies the universe by timing pulsars with extreme precision. On June 29, 2023, it announced evidence for a background of low-frequency gravitational waves passing through our galaxy, drawn from 15 years of data. Groups across North America, Europe, Asia, and Australia announced together, and the news traveled around the world. NANOGrav knew a result like this would draw people who had never heard of pulsar timing, and it needed a site ready to explain it.

At Sandbox, we started in April 2022 and had the site live ahead of the announcement. We refreshed the brand and built a site the collaboration uses both to explain itself to the public and to run its day-to-day communication. I led the strategy and design, including the updated brand.

## The result

In the week of the announcement, 41,000 people from 157 countries visited the site, and 9 in 10 of them were there for the first time. Three years on, it’s still NANOGrav’s public face. The collaboration maintains it independently, and members post their own news and events.

## Making hard science approachable

Nanohertz gravitational waves and pulsar timing aren’t everyday concepts for most of us. The site had to give newcomers a way into the science without watering it down.

Custom illustration carries the concepts that resist a photograph. An interactive glossary runs throughout, defining technical terms right in the text, so readers never have to leave the page to look one up. Each science topic gets its own short, plain-language introduction.

## A site the collaboration can run itself

NANOGrav is large and spread across many institutions. Its content changes often, so we built the site for the community to keep current on its own. Members contribute news, post events, and update their own bios, and a site editor approves what goes live. The site stays consistent, and no update waits on a developer.
